# Klets - the method, as a checklist

This is the contract for every build. It follows the SaySomethingin method as documented in `research/01-saysomethingin-method.md`. Anything in the "Extras" section is off by default and must never change the core loop.

## The core loop (every klets, every line)

1. The learner hears an **English prompt** in the narrator's voice.
2. **Silence.** A ring fills on screen. Nothing else appears: no Dutch text, no translation, no buttons other than pause.
3. The learner **says the Dutch out loud**.
4. **Voice one** says the Dutch (Flemish, female).
5. **Voice two** says the Dutch (Flemish, male).
6. A short beat, then the next prompt. No grading, no microphone, no tap required.

The pause length is set by the script per line (longer for new or long sentences) and the learner can lengthen all pauses in settings. The pause button exists and the first klets says it is fine to use it; the goal is to need it less each week.

## How a klets is scripted

- **Sentences, not words.** A new chunk is introduced once on its own (*the word for "I want" is* ... *ik wil*), then used in a full sentence within the next two prompts, then recombined with chunks the learner already has.
- **Recombination is the point.** Roughly every fourth prompt is a sentence the learner has never heard, built entirely from chunks they know.
- **Spacing is written into the script**, not left to an algorithm. A new chunk is used three times in its klets, returns in each of the next two kletsen, then about every fifth klets, each time in a new combination.
- **No grammar.** The script never explains a rule. Word order and *de/het* are learned by producing hundreds of correct sentences. The only spoken asides are the narrator's encouragement and, at most, one short cultural note per klets when a phrase needs it (*one kiss, not three*).
- **Five minutes, 15 to 20 prompts.** Four kletsen cover what one 30-minute SaySomethingin challenge covers.
- **Listening at speed.** From klets 8 of each level, a two-minute listening file of known sentences at 1.5x, later 2x, is offered after the klets. The learner is told they do not need to understand it.

## Rules the learner is told in klets 1

- Say it out loud. Thinking it does not count.
- Do not write anything down. Do not look anything up.
- Do not repeat a klets. Three in four right is enough. Everything comes back on its own.
- Mistakes are the lesson working. Everyone freezes at first.
- Use the pause if you need it. Try to need it less.

## Progress and habit

- One klets a day, pointed at by the daily email, carried over until finished. No streaks, no counters of missed days.
- Progress is shown as minutes spoken and the next *brevet*. A brevet is earned by finishing the level's free-flow klets, not by a test.
- The learner never sees a score.

## Extras (off by default, never in the loop)

- **Transcript peek.** After a klets, and only after, the learner can open the Dutch text of what they just said. Off until Level A2, and the learner switches it on themselves.
- **Herhaling.** A two-minute optional review block after a klets, chosen by a lightweight scheduler from chunks the learner tapped "that one got me" on. The tap is a single button during the pause and does nothing to the flow.
- **Record and compare.** Hear your own attempt next to the native voice. No scoring, nothing leaves the device. Later phase.
- **The written email scene at B2** in the Ouders strand, because school communication is written. The only place the course touches reading and writing.

## What the build must be checked against

- During a klets the screen shows no Dutch text. Ever.
- The learner can finish a klets without touching the screen.
- Every Dutch line is heard from two different Flemish speakers.
- A klets script contains at least one recombined sentence per four prompts and reuses chunks from earlier kletsen.
- Closing the app mid-klets and reopening resumes at the same prompt.
- The daily email points at the same klets until it is finished.
