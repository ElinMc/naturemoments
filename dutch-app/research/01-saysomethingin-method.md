# Research briefing 1: the SaySomethingin (SSi) method, product and community

Research date: 21 September 2026. Around 50 web searches. Direct page fetches to saysomethingin.com, its forum, nation.cymru, Trustpilot, the app stores and Amazon were blocked by the research sandbox's proxy, so facts below come from search-engine extracts of those pages. Items that could not be confirmed are marked **[unverified]**. Nothing is invented; where a number rests on a single secondary source it says so.

## 0. Who and what

- SaySomethingin.com Ltd (Cyf), Wales. Co-founded by **Aran Jones** (CEO, "methodology author") and **Iestyn ap Dafydd**. First SaySomethinginWelsh (SSiW) lessons recorded 2009 "in a small, damp recording studio"; company formed 2010.
- The original Welsh lessons were recorded by Aran with his wife **Catrin**: Aran gives the English, the learner speaks, Catrin then Aran say it in Welsh. Northern and Southern Welsh variants exist.
- Community "numbering in the tens of thousands" of learners over 12+ years. No audited user count found.
- Recent history: AutoMagic Welsh beta launched on the site; a new **SaySomethingin app** for iOS and Android launched **October 2024** with Welsh, French and Spanish; **June 2024** nation.cymru reported SSi "branching out to 40 new languages" and offering Welsh *from* 40 source languages; a full **Irish** course launched with Agile Cymru funding. SSi is "official Language Partner" to GlobalWelsh and says it collaborates with the Welsh Government.
- Aran Jones's books: *High Intensity Language Training: An introduction to the key principles* (Kindle, 2014) and *Some Sex and a Hill: or How to Learn Welsh in 3 Easy Pints* (memoir). He writes a regular column on nation.cymru (2025: "Learning Welsh: neurons vs emotions", "Want to speak Welsh? Make MORE mistakes (it's a superpower)", "Practice and the art of learning Welsh").

## 1. Pedagogy

**Core loop (classic "challenges").** Audio only. English prompt, deliberate silence (in the app a red ring signals "your go"), learner says the sentence aloud, two native speakers say it (in Welsh: Catrin, then Aran), learner may repeat. App-store and site copy: "from the very first lesson you're producing sentences out loud, with a pause that forces you to commit before you hear the answer... Freezing isn't failure; it's the beginning of the lesson. Everyone freezes at first."

**Full sentences, not word lists.** The HILT book description: the method centres on *production* of sentences from an L1 prompt "as opposed to simply repeating what the teacher says", and on building "a store of carefully selected sentence fragments that are generally useful in making conversation (what Aran calls **Formulaic Blocks**)" which the learner recombines "in new ways (as children do)". Chapter topics include how speaking affects synapses, "the cumulative effect of formulaic blocks", extrapolation, **rest periods** and **accelerated listening exercises**. Site copy: "no formal grammar... you'll start to use normal, grammatical sentences because of the way the course trains your brain"; the method is "like high-intensity interval training for languages".

**Rules.** No writing, no notes, no reading; do not repeat lessons; keep moving even when it feels shaky. Forum threads ("To pause, or not to pause", "Pause button or repeat lessons?") confirm learners "are encouraged not to use the pause button, but also encouraged not to repeat lessons and keep pressing on." Aran's 2013 blog post argues from their accelerated-learning trials that "repeating learnt material more often than we actually need to is almost certainly unnecessary". The widely quoted heuristic that about 80% right is enough to move on: **[unverified - found only in learner posts, not in official copy]**.

**Pause button.** Official stance is nuanced: the pause exists and beginners are told it is fine to use it to formulate the sentence, but the goal is instinctive, unpaused answers. The literal phrase "the pause button is your friend" was not found in an official source **[unverified]**.

**Mistakes.** Central and explicit. nation.cymru columns: "one of our main challenges in the first hour is to make sure you understand that making mistakes is a good thing... a sign that you're learning"; "Every single mistake is a golden moment of learning". He frames two journeys: a comparatively simple *neurological* one (forming memories) and a harder *emotional* one that is "almost always the real reason people stop learning".

**Spaced repetition and interleaving.** SSi does not market "SRS" by name; it says the course is "carefully scripted... to make sure the memory techniques embedded in it work for every single word and every single structure". Chunks reappear in new combinations across challenges; AutoMagic explicitly monitors performance and re-serves items. Forum users praise AutoMagic's "structured repetition".

**Accelerated listening.** From **Level 1 Challenge 10 onward the Listening Practices are at double speed**, with an explanation attached. Purpose: train the ear so normal speed feels slow; you are told you do not need to understand it.

**Course sizes and hours.**
- Welsh Level 1 = **25 challenges** (Challenge 25 is framed as "you are now a Welsh speaker"). Level 2 = 25 challenges. Level 3 exists, probably 25 **[not independently confirmed]**. There is no Level 4.
- Older "Course 1/2/3" (26 lessons each) were replaced by "Levels"; Level 1 was a redesign giving "more immediately useful conversational material".
- Each classic challenge is about **30 minutes**. SSi's 2019 blog "How long does it take to learn Welsh?": about **100 intensive hours** on the tasks leads to "decent conversations".
- Proficiency claims: Level 1 leads to "Welsh speaker" and first real conversations; the 6-month intensive "guarantees you'll be having confident conversations in 6 months" at 3 to 4 hours a week. No CEFR mapping found in official copy **[unverified]**.

**Programmes.** *6 Minutes a Day* (about 42 minutes a week, email-driven); *6-Month Welsh Speaker course* (3 to 4 hours a week, weekly emailed tasks, "Leap in at the deep end"); a *Jump in the Deep End* course for people who finished Level 1; *Bwtcamp*, a Saturday-to-Saturday residential week, max 10 people, £349 plus £10 booking, with "no English" as the premise; an *Intensive Language Learning Programme* offering one-to-one accelerated coaching.

**AutoMagic.** SSi's blog "The story behind AutoMagic": a streaming, sentence-by-sentence player would generate the per-learner data needed for an adaptive "Tutor"; it took about 3 years to build. The Tutor lets learners "go at different speeds... and choose how long they spend". Learners can adjust delivery speed; progress is shown in hours and martial-arts belts (white, yellow, yellow with stripes, orange, up to black belt), after which content continues. "Aran's asides" are short spoken interjections between items. Forum bug threads mention sessions getting stuck and asides looping. **Session length in fixed 5-minute chunks: not confirmed**; official copy only says sessions are user-chosen and can be short.

**Evidence cited.** SSi cites "the latest research in language learning", the neuroscience of synapses, its own accelerated-learning trials and learner outcomes. **No peer-reviewed study** of SSi itself was found.

## 2. Product and UX

- **Player.** Audio-first. Prompt in English, timed silence with a visual cue (red ring), then native-speaker answers; a pause control; in AutoMagic a speed setting and hours/belt progress. Release notes mention "a progress bar on the Learning Screen". Screenshots could not be viewed, so button labels are **[unverified]**.
- **Progress display.** Belts plus hours (AutoMagic); challenge tick-lists on the account dashboard for the emailed courses.
- **Tone of voice.** Warm, informal, first-person, encouraging, slightly cheeky (post title "Would I be rude enough to tell you that you're doing it all wrong? Oh, yes!"; Spanish landing page headline "It's not your fault you don't speak Spanish yet."). Emphasis on confidence and mistakes as progress.
- **Visual design, typography and colours.** Could not be inspected **[unverified]**.
- **Pricing.** App: free up to the start of Orange Belt in every language; one monthly auto-renewing subscription unlocks everything to Black Belt on all courses. The 6-Minute Welsh signup page cites **£10 a month**, cancel anytime, with a first-month money-back guarantee for learners who did all the tasks and still lack confidence. Older web courses: Lesson 1 free without registration, Lesson 2 needs registration, Lesson 3 onward paid or unlocked by social share.
- **Languages.** Welsh (N/S), Spanish, French, Irish (full), Manx, Cornish, Dutch (older "Course 1" and "Tourist course"; "Dutch course 3" discussed on the forum), Italian (beta), plus Welsh for Spanish speakers. June 2024 announcement of 40 new languages via AutoMagic. **Dutch: yes**, a course exists (en.saysomethingin.com/dutch/course1), but it is in the older format and its status in the new app is unclear.
- **Reviews and criticisms.** Trustpilot 4.4/5 on about 95 reviews. Praise: speaks from day one, "learned more in 4 months than 10 years of Duolingo", belts motivating. Criticisms: no reading or writing (visual learners struggle; Welsh spelling later feels alien), no grammar or mutation explanations, pace and overwhelm ("definitely not for beginners"; a 6-month learner "hit a plateau"), AutoMagic bugs (looping, stuck sessions, migration without warning), long sentences in mid-level AutoMagic with too little time.

## 3. Community and motivation loops

- **Forum** (Discourse): categories for Welsh, Spanish, other languages, meetups; Aran and staff post directly; long-running "Why Welsh? Why SSiW? What's your story?" thread.
- **Slack "Welsh Speaking Practice"**: open to all levels, audio and video practice with learners and the SSi team, Zoom events.
- **Email**: the 6-Minute and 6-Month courses are delivered as regular emailed challenges with dashboard tick-offs.
- **Learner Stories** page, nation.cymru features, Bwtcamp diaries, Facebook page; Aran's columns act as method evangelism.
- Motivational framing: belts, hours counter, "you're a Welsh speaker" at Challenge 25, the money-back guarantee and the explicit emotional-support narrative.

## 4. Academic framing and comparisons

- **Automaticity and chunking**: Formulaic Blocks map to Wray's formulaic sequences; the design goal is proceduralised, automatic retrieval rather than declarative rules.
- **Output hypothesis (Swain)**: SSi is output-first, noticing gaps via forced production before hearing the model. This is closer to Swain than to Krashen's input-only stance, though SSi also supplies dense input via the answers and listening practices.
- **Nation's four strands**: SSi is heavy on meaning-focused output and fluency development (speed, no pausing, double-speed listening), light on meaning-focused input beyond the listening files, and deliberately near-zero on language-focused learning (no grammar), which is the source of the main criticism.
- **Pimsleur**: shares the anticipation ("challenge and response") principle and graduated-interval recall; Pimsleur is slower, 30-minute lessons, repeat until 70 to 80%.
- **Michel Thomas and Language Transfer**: teacher-plus-students eavesdropping model with explicit but light structural explanation; SSi drops the explanations and the classroom framing.

| | SSi | Michel Thomas | Pimsleur | Language Transfer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | English prompt, pause, learner speaks, two native speakers; formulaic chunks recombined; no grammar; belts | Teacher plus two students, listener as "third student"; building-block explanations | Native host; anticipation prompts; graduated-interval recall | Teacher plus one student ("Thinking Method") |
| Session length | About 30 min classic; AutoMagic user-chosen | Foundation about 8 h | 30 min a lesson, 30 lessons a level (about 15 h) | 5 to 15 min tracks |
| Speaking vs listening | Speaking first; separate double-speed listening files | Listening plus prompted speaking, explanation-heavy | Balanced drills | Prompted speaking with explanation |
| Cost | Free to Orange Belt; about £10 a month | About $100 to 150 a course | About $20 a month | Free |
