# Research briefing 2: the CEFR (ERK) applied to Dutch as a second language, A1 to B2

Research date: 21 September 2026. Over 30 web searches. Most direct page fetches (coe.int, staatsexamensnt2.nl, taalunie.org, cnavt.org, divosa.nl, nt2.nl, coutinho.nl) were blocked by the research sandbox's proxy, so most facts come from search-engine extracts. Where wording or a number could not be verified verbatim it is marked **[unverified]** or given as a range with sources named.

## 1. CEFR levels A1 to B2: official descriptors

### 1.1 Global scale (verbatim; unchanged in the 2020 Companion Volume)

- **A1 (Breakthrough):** "Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases aimed at the satisfaction of needs of a concrete type. Can introduce him/herself and others and can ask and answer questions about personal details such as where he/she lives, people he/she knows and things he/she has. Can interact in a simple way provided the other person talks slowly and clearly and is prepared to help."
- **A2 (Waystage):** "Can understand sentences and frequently used expressions related to areas of most immediate relevance (e.g. very basic personal and family information, shopping, local geography, employment). Can communicate in simple and routine tasks requiring a simple and direct exchange of information on familiar and routine matters. Can describe in simple terms aspects of his/her background, immediate environment and matters in areas of immediate need."
- **B1 (Threshold):** "Can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure, etc. Can deal with most situations likely to arise whilst travelling in an area where the language is spoken. Can produce simple connected text on topics which are familiar or of personal interest. Can describe experiences and events, dreams, hopes & ambitions and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans."
- **B2 (Vantage):** "Can understand the main ideas of complex text on both concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in his/her field of specialisation. Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain for either party. Can produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects and explain a viewpoint on a topical issue giving the advantages and disadvantages of various options."

The 2020 Companion Volume replaced "native speakers" with "users of the target language" and "he/she" with "they" in many scales, and added a Pre-A1 level and "plus" levels.

### 1.2 Overall oral production (Companion Volume wording)

- **A1:** "Can produce simple, mainly isolated phrases about people and places."
- **A2:** "Can give a simple description or presentation of people, living or working conditions, daily routines, likes/dislikes, etc. as a short series of simple phrases and sentences linked into a list."
- **B1:** "Can reasonably fluently sustain a straightforward description of one of a variety of subjects within their field of interest, presenting it as a linear sequence of points."
- **B2:** "Can give clear, detailed descriptions and presentations on a wide range of subjects related to their field of interest, expanding and supporting ideas with subsidiary points and relevant examples."

### 1.3 Overall oral interaction

- **A1 [standard text, verbatim retrieval blocked]:** "Can interact in a simple way but communication is totally dependent on repetition at a slower rate, rephrasing and repair. Can ask and answer simple questions, initiate and respond to simple statements in areas of immediate need or on very familiar topics."
- **A2 [as above]:** "Can interact with reasonable ease in structured situations and short conversations, provided the other person helps if necessary. Can manage simple, routine exchanges without undue effort; can ask and answer questions and exchange ideas and information on familiar topics in predictable everyday situations."
- **B1 (confirmed):** "Can communicate with some confidence on familiar routine and non-routine matters related to their interests and professional field. Can exchange, check and confirm information, deal with less routine situations and explain why something is a problem."
- **B2 (confirmed):** "Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction, and sustained relationships with native speakers quite possible without imposing strain on either party."

### 1.4 Overall oral comprehension [standard text, verbatim retrieval blocked]

- **A1:** "Can follow speech that is very slow and carefully articulated, with long pauses for them to assimilate meaning."
- **A2:** "Can understand enough to be able to meet needs of a concrete type, provided people speak slowly and clearly. Can understand phrases and expressions related to areas of most immediate priority."
- **B1:** "Can understand the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure, etc., including short narratives."
- **B2:** "Can understand standard spoken language, live or broadcast, on both familiar and unfamiliar topics normally encountered in personal, social, academic or vocational life. Only extreme background noise, inadequate discourse structure and/or idiomatic usage influence the ability to understand."

The Taalunie hosts a Dutch-language version of the descriptors at erk-nederlands.taalunie.org (the "ERK"); the Dutch skill labels used in NT2 are *luisteren, lezen, gesprekken voeren, spreken, schrijven*.

## 2. Dutch-specific frameworks and exams

### 2.1 Raamwerk NT2 (2002)
Authored by Kaatje Dalderop (Cito), Ellie Liemberg and Franke Teunisse (Bureau ICE), published by the BVE Raad and distributed by CINOP as part of the Portfolio NT2 project for the Ministry of OCW. It elaborates the 2001 CEFR for adult NT2 with the same A1 to C2 scale and five skills. Both the inburgeringsexamen and the Staatsexamens NT2 are based on it.

### 2.2 Staatsexamen NT2
- **Programma I = B1**, for people heading for work or vocational training at mbo level; **Programma II = B2**, for hbo or university study or higher-level jobs.
- Four separately certificated components: Lezen, Luisteren, Spreken, Schrijven; all on computer; a diploma needs all four; a failed part can be retaken alone.
- Durations reported by exam-training sites: Lezen 100 min; Luisteren 90 min; Spreken about 25 min; Schrijven two parts of 60 min **[secondary sources]**.
- Bureau ICE makes Lezen and Schrijven; Cito makes Luisteren and Spreken; under the College voor Toetsen en Examens (CvTE); DUO administers. CvTE publishes an "Aanvullende woordenlijst Staatsexamen NT2" (renewed January 2024). Free practice exams: nt2-oefenomgeving.facet.onl.

### 2.3 Inburgering: Wet inburgering 2013 (A2) versus Wet inburgering 2021 (B1)
- **Old law (Wi2013):** exams at **A2** in the four skills plus KNM (Kennis van de Nederlandse Maatschappij) and ONA (Oriëntatie op de Nederlandse Arbeidsmarkt).
- **New law (Wi2021, in force 1 January 2022):** municipalities steer the trajectory; the default aim is **B1**. Three routes: **B1-route**, **Onderwijsroute** (bridging to mbo/hbo/wo, also B1 minimum) and **Z-route** (zelfredzaamheid, no exam). Obligations: central exam (Lezen, Luisteren, Schrijven, Spreken, KNM), plus the Module Arbeidsmarkt en Participatie and the Participatieverklaringstraject; three-year deadline. In the B1-route, **afschalen** to A2 is permitted only after "aantoonbare inspanning"; secondary sources cite **at least 600 hours of lessons** as the threshold **[confirm in the Besluit inburgering 2021]**.

### 2.4 CNaVT (Certificaat Nederlands als Vreemde Taal)
A Taalunie project; exams developed by the Centrum voor Taal en Onderwijs at KU Leuven; one sitting a year (May); fully digital from May 2026. Profiles: **Maatschappelijk Informeel (INFO) A2**; **Maatschappelijk Formeel (FORM) B1**; **Zakelijk Professioneel (PROF) B2**; **Educatief Startbekwaam (STRT) B2** (admission to Dutch-language higher education); **Educatief Professioneel (EDUP) C1**.

### 2.5 Guided learning hours
- **Cambridge English (cumulative from zero, for English):** A2 180 to 200; B1 350 to 400; B2 500 to 600; C1 700 to 800. A Cambridge blog gives roughly 100 to 150 hours to move A1 to A2. Rule of thumb: about 200 hours a level.
- **Alliance Française** (French): "each level about 180 to 220 h"; not consistent across branches.
- **Dutch providers (no official Taalunie or OCW hour norm found):** Radboud in'to Languages runs courses per half-level with two 1h45 sessions a week over a semester plus 8 to 10 h a week self-study. UvA INTT: six levels from 0 to B2; semi-intensive is 2 x 3 h class plus about 6 h self-study a week. Delftse methode: *Nederlands voor buitenlanders* (43 lessons, about 2,000 words) reaches A2; *Tweede ronde* A2 to B1; *Derde ronde* B1 to B2. The 600 class-hours threshold before A2 afschalen implies the state treats 600 h as a reasonable minimum for B1.
- **Verdict:** the A2 to B2 numbers commonly quoted for Dutch are Cambridge's English figures transferred. No Dutch institution publishes equivalent cumulative norms. Cite them as "Cambridge guided learning hours, commonly transferred to Dutch". The A1 figure of 80 to 100 hours is an inference, not a published number.

## 4. Typical NT2 grammar syllabus per level

The site *Grammatica voor NT2-docenten* compared *Nederlands in gang/in actie/op niveau/naar perfectie*, *Code(+)*, *De Opmaat/Sprong/Finale* and *Contact!* and notes that methods disagree (some introduce subordinate clauses at A1, others at B1). The commonly observed progression:

- **A1** (*Nederlands in gang* lessons 1 to 9; Taaltalent 1; Code+ 1; Contact! 1): pronunciation and spelling (long and short vowels, open and closed syllables); personal pronouns; present tense including zijn and hebben; **de/het** and **plural** (-en/-s); **negation niet/geen**; **main-clause word order (verb second) and inversion**; question words; **modal verbs** (kunnen, moeten, mogen, willen, zullen); **er is/er zijn**; numbers, time, adjective inflection (-e); possessives; imperative; diminutives introduced early in some methods.
- **A2** (*Nederlands in gang* lessons 10 to 18; Taaltalent 2; Delftse basiscursus): **perfectum** (hebben/zijn plus participle, irregular participles); **imperfectum**; **separable verbs**; **subordinate clauses** with verb-final order after *omdat, als, dat, of, wanneer, hoewel*; comparative and superlative; reflexive verbs; *om ... te* plus infinitive; demonstratives; object pronouns; *er* (locative and with quantities); future with *gaan/zullen*.
- **B1** (*Nederlands in actie*; Taaltalent 3; Contact! nieuw 3): the publisher's list covers conjugation across tenses, modal verbs, **conjunctions and subordinate-clause formation**, **indirect speech**, **(om) te plus infinitive**, **separable verbs**, **the functions of *er*** (including *er* plus preposition: *erover, ervan*), **relative clauses** (*die/dat/wie/wat/waar* plus preposition), **functions of *zou/zouden*** (conditional, polite request, hearsay), and **recognition and use of the passive**. Also plusquamperfectum, *te* plus infinitive after *zonder/door/in plaats van*, conjunctions of contrast, cause and purpose.
- **B2** (*Nederlands op niveau*; Delftse *Derde ronde*; LINK+ B1>B2): consolidation plus nuance: complex subordination, connectors for argumentation, passive in all tenses, participial and infinitive constructions, formal written register (letters, reports), fixed expressions and idiom, discourse markers, register choice (u/je), text structure for Staatsexamen II writing. Reference grammars: *Zichtbaar Nederlands* (Coutinho) and dutchgrammar.com.

Coutinho's series: *Nederlands in gang* (0 to A2, 18 lessons), *Nederlands in actie* (A2 to B1), *Nederlands op niveau* (B1 to B2), *Nederlands naar perfectie* (B2 to C1). Code+ (ThiemeMeulenhoff) and Contact! (Intertaal/Klett) run 1 to 3 over A1 to B1.

## 6. Pronunciation and register: what learners struggle with

- **g/ch:** voiceless velar or uvular fricative. The Netherlands (especially the Randstad) uses a *harde g* at or near the uvula; the southern provinces and Flanders use a *zachte g*. Most coursebooks and the Piper nl_NL voices model the hard g. *sch* = [sx]; *-isch* = [is].
- **ui, eu, ij/ei, au/ou:** *ui* has no English equivalent and is the most-cited difficulty; *eu* and *uu* are front rounded vowels; *ij* and *ei* are homophones, as are *au* and *ou*. Also vowel-length contrasts (*man/maan*, *bos/boos*).
- **r:** several equally standard variants: alveolar trill, uvular, and the *Gooise r* (a bunched approximant in syllable coda, prestige in Randstad media). Learners may choose; consistency matters more than variant.
- **u versus je/jij:** *je/jij* is the default in the Netherlands, including many workplaces; *u* with strangers, older people, officials, customers and in formal letters. *Alstublieft/dank u wel* pair with *u*; *alsjeblieft/dank je wel* with *je*.
- **Netherlands versus Flanders:** Flemish speakers use *u* far more; the colloquial pronoun *gij/ge* is widespread in Flanders and absent from Netherlands Dutch; Flanders has the soft g, generally trilled r, a distinct "tussentaal" and vocabulary differences (*plezant, goesting, schoon* = mooi, *tas* = kopje, *appelsien*). Both varieties are standard Dutch per the Taalunie.
- **Recommendation for a Netherlands-focused course:** teach *Standaardnederlands* as spoken in the Netherlands (Randstad broadcast norm): hard g, *je/jij* as default with explicit *u* rules, tolerate any r, use nl-NL TTS voices, and add a short awareness module on Flemish later rather than mixing varieties in input. This matches the Staatsexamen NT2 and the inburgeringsexamen.
