# Klets - recording guide for the Dutch voices

Two lists to record: `recording-A1.md` (about 190 lines, for the A1 learner) and `recording-A2.md` (about 165 lines, for the A2 learner). Together about 355 lines, roughly 35 to 40 minutes of recording per speaker including pauses. Ideally two speakers, one woman and one man, each recording everything. One speaker is enough to start.

The English prompts are not for the Dutch speaker. Elin records those separately (same method, same lists, English column) or the app shows them as text in the trial. Either works.

## Who
- A native speaker of Netherlands Dutch, hard g, Randstad or general standard accent. Not Flemish for this trial, so the two voices sound like one country.
- Someone who can read a line naturally as if talking to a friend who is learning, not as a newsreader and not over-articulated.

## Where and with what
- A quiet room with soft furnishings (curtains, sofa, bed). No fridge, no traffic window, no fan. Phones on silent.
- Simplest good option: an iPhone with Voice Memos. First set Settings, Voice Memos, Audio Quality to **Lossless**. Hold or prop the phone about 15 to 20 cm from the mouth, slightly to one side so breath does not hit the microphone.
- Android: the built-in Recorder on Pixel, or Easy Voice Recorder set to **WAV**.
- A laptop with a USB microphone and Audacity is better still, if available. Export as WAV, 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz, 16 bit or 24 bit, mono.
- Same room, same device, same distance for every session, so all the files match.

## How to record
1. Before the full session, record the first three lines of A1 klets 1, upload them (see below) and wait for my check. Five minutes now saves redoing an hour.
2. **One file per klets per speaker.** Read the Dutch lines of that klets in order, top to bottom.
3. Leave about **two seconds of silence** between lines. I split the file on those silences, so the gap matters more than anything else.
4. Do not read the line numbers and do not read the English.
5. If a line comes out wrong, pause two seconds and say it again. I take the last version. Write the line number on the checklist at the bottom of the list file so I know which lines have a retake.
6. Natural, clear pace. Not slow. The learner will hear these hundreds of times, so they should sound like real Dutch, not like a lesson.
7. Lines marked **(u)** are formal, spoken by shop staff or to them. A slightly more neutral, polite tone. Lines marked **(vraag)** are questions, so let the intonation rise.
8. Lines that are single words or short chunks (*ik wil*, *een beetje*) are said on their own, plainly, as you would if someone asked "how do you say 'I want'?".
9. Keep some energy. Recording sixty lines in a row goes flat; take a break every two kletsen and have water nearby.
10. If a phrase feels unnatural or wrong to the speaker, that is valuable. Say the natural version instead and note the change on the checklist. A native ear beats my script.

## File names
`A1-03-anne.m4a` means level A1, klets 3, speaker Anne. Use the speaker's first name. WAV, M4A (lossless or high quality AAC) or FLAC are all fine. Please no MP3 and no WhatsApp voice notes, both compress too hard.

## Handing the files over
Upload the files into this repository under `dutch-app/audio/raw/` on the branch `claude/dutch-learning-app-ygqjqu` using the GitHub website (Add file, Upload files). About 20 files per speaker, roughly 40 to 80 MB in total. If that is awkward, any EU file share works (Proton Drive, Infomaniak kDrive, a Nextcloud link) and you paste the link here.

## What I do with them
Split on silence, trim the ends, match the loudness of every line, convert to a small web format, name each file by its line ID and check every klets has every line. Anything missing or unclear comes back as a short retake list.

## Rights
Ask the speaker to agree in a message that the recordings may be used in the app, including a paid version later. A sentence is enough for the household trial; a short written agreement before any public launch.
